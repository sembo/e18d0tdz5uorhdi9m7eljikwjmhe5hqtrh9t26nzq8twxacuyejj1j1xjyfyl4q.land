# The Last Acres of the Internet
## About
This project was created to mark exonemo's 30th anniversary, as a tribute to the net.art pioneers who once broke ground on unclaimed land. Over these past thirty years, the landscape of the internet has changed in many ways. Long after the dazzling dawn of JODI's flickering, broken ASCII art, the center of the internet has shifted to social media, and expression centered on the website itself has faded into the background, as if we now stood in the twilight of dusk.

In today's internet, parceled out and developed down to the last corner, we found one last untouched paradise: domain names made of meaningless, random strings, 63 characters long*1. On that domain, we built a vast wilderness of HTML made from random clusters of characters.

We sell this wilderness HTML together with the domain, through a range of Agents, each in its own way. How you buy differs by Agent, but every buyer receives ownership of the domain along with the HTML. Owners are expected to cover the domain's renewal fees from the following year on, to keep the land. The wilderness HTML file is also free for buyers to modify as they like, since the appeal of owning land lies in developing it yourself and building whatever you want. So what will you build on this unclaimed land? A castle of your own? A shopping mall? A secret laboratory? It's entirely up to you.

The wilderness HTML is a piece of "Unicode art" by exonemo, generated using the domain name as its seed. Within it, characters of many kinds, drawn at random from Unicode, cluster together to form patterns like terrain. Among them are many square glyphs known as "tofu," marking spots where no character has yet been assigned. Every year, the Unicode Consortium adds anywhere from a few hundred to more than ten thousand new characters to these unassigned spots. Which means the wilderness HTML keeps changing its face, as characters gradually settle into the tofu over time. It keeps shifting, the way weeds grow over a plot of land left untended.

When the internet first began spreading widely, everyone shared the same dream: wondering what could be done with this uncharted land. Now that feeling is fading. Remembering that old excitement, we hold hope for an internet that still holds far more unclaimed land than we think, waiting for new pioneers.

*1 The maximum number of characters that can be registered as a second-level domain (the "ABC" part of ABC.com).
